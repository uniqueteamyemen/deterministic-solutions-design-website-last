# V08 — White or Yellow?

**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 15 seconds, silent visual master plus optional narration/caption.  
**Status:** Script approved for generation review; not approved for publication.  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate that an apparently forced binary can be a question with an untested premise.

## Story beats

| Time | Visual action | Optional narration / caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5s | Two folded T-shirts, one white and one yellow, sit beside a beach bag. A hand hovers between them. | “White or yellow?” |
| 5–10s | The hand stops. The shirts remain folded as the bag opens to reveal a towel, sunscreen, and swimwear. | “Is that the real question?” |
| 10–15s | The person walks toward the sea in simple beachwear; the shirts remain behind. One oxide point aligns with a clean horizon reference line. | “Test the premise.” |

## Generation brief

Create a 15-second vertical editorial campaign video set at a calm beach in early morning. An adult beachgoer, shown only from a respectful mid-distance and without a recognizable likeness, pauses between a folded white T-shirt and folded yellow T-shirt beside a natural canvas bag. They reconsider the choice, leave both shirts folded, take a towel, sunscreen, and simple swimwear, then walk toward the shoreline. End on a clean horizon line with one small oxide-orange point. Style: DS&D Reference Mark, porcelain sand, graphite shadows, muted moss, one oxide point, calm observational pacing, non-sexual and non-glamorous beachwear. No readable on-screen text, no brand logos, no celebrity likeness, no sexualized framing, no neon or generic AI-tech visuals.

## Post caption

> White or yellow?
>
> Sometimes the useful answer is: neither.
>
> Before optimizing the options, inspect the question that made them seem necessary.
