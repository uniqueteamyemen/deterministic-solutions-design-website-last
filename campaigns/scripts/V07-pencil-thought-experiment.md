# V07 — The Pencil Is Not the Lesson

**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 15 seconds, silent visual master plus optional narration/caption.  
**Status:** Script approved for generation review; not approved for publication.  
**Purpose:** Challenge the reflex to call an answer “simple” before identifying the real operating constraint.

## Story beats

| Time | Visual action | Optional narration / caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5s | A precise graphite desk. A hand considers an elaborate chrome writing instrument, then pauses over an ordinary wooden pencil. | “The easy story is tempting.” |
| 5–10s | The hand places both objects beside a clear safety checklist. One constraint line is marked with a single oxide point. | “But the real constraint matters.” |
| 10–15s | The pencil and pen fade into a single reference line moving toward the oxide point. | “Solve the actual problem.” |

## Generation brief

Create a 15-second vertical editorial campaign video for a systems-design office. A calm adult hand at a porcelain-mineral desk considers an ordinary unbranded wooden pencil and an abstract, unbranded precision writing instrument. The hand does not choose either immediately; it reveals a simple safety checklist with one meaningful oxide-orange reference point. End with both objects becoming a quiet graphite line leading to the orange point. Visual language: DS&D Reference Mark, porcelain paper, graphite structure, muted moss, one oxide point, restrained river-blue reference line, calm editorial engineering record. No readable on-screen text, no logos, no NASA imagery, no spacecraft, no brands, no celebrity or real-person likeness, no neon, no circuit-board visuals.

## Post caption

> The point is not “always choose the pencil.”
>
> The point is: identify the actual constraint before deciding what simple means.
>
> **What appears compulsory may not be.**
