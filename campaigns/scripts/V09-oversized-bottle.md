# V09 — The Smaller Can Is Not the Only Option

**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 15 seconds, silent visual master plus optional narration/caption.  
**Status:** Script approved for generation review; not approved for publication.  
**Purpose:** Show that a familiar next step can hide a simpler available path.

## Story beats

| Time | Visual action | Optional narration / caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5s | At a picnic table, an adult reaches for an unopened small generic soda can. A chilled oversized generic bottle is already within reach. | “The next step feels obvious.” |
| 5–10s | The hand pauses, looks at the bottle, and pours a glass from it instead of opening the can. | “But is it necessary?” |
| 10–15s | The unopened can and used bottle sit beside a clean shared glass. A graphite line passes through one oxide point. | “Inspect the available path.” |

## Generation brief

Create a 15-second vertical editorial campaign video at a simple outdoor picnic table. An adult reaches toward an unopened small generic cola can, pauses, notices a large chilled generic soft-drink bottle already available, and pours from the large bottle into a reusable glass instead. End with the unopened small can, the used unbranded bottle, and a single oxide-orange reference point connected by a restrained graphite line. Visual language: DS&D Reference Mark, porcelain linen, graphite tableware, muted moss foliage, one oxide point, calm practical observation. No readable on-screen text, no Pepsi logo, no Coca-Cola logo, no third-party brand marks, no celebrity likeness, no neon or circuit imagery.

## Post caption

> The smaller can was not the only option.
>
> The useful question was never: “How do we open it?”
>
> It was: “What do we actually need right now?”
